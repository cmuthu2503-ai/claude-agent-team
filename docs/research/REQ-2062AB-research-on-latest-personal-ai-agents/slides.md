# Slide 3: Why This Matters Now
- 15% of daily work decisions will be made by agentic AI in 2026 (Gartner)
- 40% of enterprise apps will ship task-specific agents — up from <5% in 2025
- Personal-agent surface = next billion-user battleground
- The shift: "drafts a reply" → "sends the reply, books the meeting, files the expense"

Speaker notes: The economic case is no longer about answering questions faster — it's about removing whole categories of work. That's why every hyperscaler is now racing into this space.

---

# Slide 4: The Big Split — Chat-First vs. Action-First
- **Chat-first**: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini — great reasoning, you drive
- **Action-first**: Lindy, Rahi, Vellum, Sai, Zapier Agents — agent drives, you supervise
- Every 2026 roundup uses this taxonomy
- Differentiation has moved from model quality to action reliability

Speaker notes: This is the single most important framing in the report. If you only remember one thing: the market split into two tiers, and the interesting innovation is happening on the action-first side.

---

# Slide 5: The New Rubric — Memory + Proactivity
- "Does it remember you?" and "Does it reach out unprompted?"
- Vellum, Lindy, Rahi all lead with proactive reach-outs
- ChatGPT and Perplexity get downgraded in 2026 reviews for being passive
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the de-facto integration standard

Speaker notes: Memory and proactivity are what separate a 2025 chatbot from a 2026 personal agent. If a tool waits for you to open it, it's competing on yesterday's rubric.

---

# Slide 6: Big Tech Wakes Up — Remy & Hatch
- **Google Remy** — internal Gemini testing, May 2026; killed Project Mariner May 4
- **Meta Hatch** — Instagram-embedded, beta by end-June 2026
- Triggered partly by OpenClaw (viral open-source agent, ex-OpenAI engineer)
- Public debut expected: Remy at Google I/O 2026 (late May)

Speaker notes: This is the news of the past two weeks. Both companies are racing to embed personal agents at billion-user scale, which will commoditize many features that startups currently charge $30–50/month for. That's a real timing consideration for any purchase decision.

---

# Slide 7: Today's Leaderboard
- **Lindy** — consensus #1 independent agent (UX, no-code, lives in your tools)
- **Claude (Pro + Cowork)** — strongest reasoning + computer-use combo
- **ChatGPT (Plus + Agent Mode)** — best raw model; agent mode "hit-or-miss"
- **Zapier Agents** — best for power users with existing automation
- **Rahi, Vellum, Sai** — credible challengers on memory and computer-use

Speaker notes: Lindy stands out because it goes to your tools instead of asking you to come to it. That's why every reviewer — including its competitors — ranks it at or near the top.

# Slide 8: Head-to-Head Scorecard
- ChatGPT Plus — 7.0/10 (great reasoning, passive)
- Claude Pro/Cowork — 7.6/10 (strong all-rounder)
- **Lindy — 8.4/10 (UX & proactivity leader)**
- **Rahi (Arahi AI) — 8.3/10 (memory & integrations leader)**
- Google Gemini — 6.7/10 (good if you live in Workspace; wait for Remy)

Speaker notes: Weighted across seven criteria: reasoning, action, memory, integrations, proactivity, price, and privacy. Lindy and Rahi pull ahead because the rubric now favors action over raw model quality.

---

# Slide 9: Pricing Reality Check
- Chat-first pro tiers: ~$20/month (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity)
- Action-first platforms: $30–50/month (Lindy, Zapier, Motion, Superhuman)
- Gemini Advanced: $9.99–16.80/month (cheapest serious option)
- DeepSeek's cost reductions pushed reasoning prices down across the board
- Payback math: ~1 hour saved/week covers a $50/month bill

Speaker notes: This is an obvious-ROI category for any salaried professional. If a tool saves you even one hour a week, it pays for itself at typical knowledge-worker rates.

---

# Slide 10: The Recommendation
- **Pair a chat-first agent with an action-first agent**
- Suggested combo: Claude Pro ($20) + Lindy (~$50) = ~$70/month
- Run a 30-day pilot; measure hours saved; rollback if <3 hrs/week
- Keep agents in read-only / draft mode for irreversible actions until trust is earned

Speaker notes: This is the default recommendation for most knowledge workers. Don't try to find one agent that does everything — pair a thinker with a doer.

---

# Slide 11: When NOT to Follow the Default
- **All-in on Google Workspace?** Wait for Remy at Google I/O — Gemini Advanced may be enough
- **Privacy-critical work?** Use OpenClaw (self-hosted) or Claude with strict scoping
- **Developer / power user?** Build on Mastra, CrewAI, or LangGraph
- **Not in pain today?** Wait 3–6 months — Remy and Hatch may commoditize this

Speaker notes: The default recommendation is wrong for these four audiences. Be honest about which one you are before signing up for anything.

---

# Slide 12: Adoption Playbook & Next Steps
- Audit your top 5 weekly time-sinks; map to specialist agents
- Pilot one paid agent for 30 days; measure recovered hours
- Set up MCP + permission scoping from day one
- Re-evaluate after Google I/O 2026 and Meta Hatch beta (end-June)
- Verify pricing & tier availability before committing — the market changes monthly

Speaker notes: Close on the playbook. The decision isn't permanent — agents are cheap and switchable. The cost of NOT piloting one is much higher than the cost of trying one and dropping it.
