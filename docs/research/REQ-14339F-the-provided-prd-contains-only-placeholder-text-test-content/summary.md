# Executive Summary

**Situation.** A PRD submitted to the engineering team contains only the placeholder string *"Test content patch."* It carries no problem statement, user definition, scope, or success criteria, and cannot be used to drive design, estimation, or build. Acting on it as-is would guarantee rework.

**Recommended action.** Do not attempt to reconstruct intent from the title or metadata. Instead, return a single, structured intake artifact to the product owner that combines (a) a batched set of 22 clarifying questions grouped into six sections — Problem & Context, Users & Roles, Goals & Scope, Success Criteria, Constraints, and Risks — and (b) a fill-in PRD template with required (✱) and optional fields. Ask for a 48-hour turnaround and offer a 15-minute review call to pressure-test the answers.

**Why this approach.** A structured template outperforms open-ended "please clarify" requests because forced-choice fields produce sharper answers from time-pressed POs. Batching questions into one document — rather than drip-feeding them over Slack — collapses clarification from days to one round-trip, creates an audit trail (the completed template *becomes* the canonical PRD), and surfaces hidden assumptions (such as missing success metrics) before engineering invests. The six required sections map directly to standard PM frameworks (Cagan, Atlassian, Amazon PR/FAQ), so missing any one is a known failure mode.

**Risks and guardrails.** The template can feel bureaucratic; mitigate by framing it as *"a 5-minute form to unblock your team"* and pre-filling any fields you can infer. For genuinely trivial work (copy changes, config tweaks), skip the template and confirm in Slack. If a hard deadline is in play and the PO is unavailable, escalate to their delegate rather than waiting. Critically, before sending the questionnaire, check the document's version history — the real PRD may have been accidentally overwritten and recoverable from a prior commit.

**Next steps.** (1) Research/PM lead sends Deliverable A + B today, CCing the engineering lead. (2) Engineering lead moves the ticket to *Blocked: Awaiting PRD* and pauses estimation. (3) PO confirms within 2 business days whether the placeholder was a mistake and whether a real draft exists elsewhere. (4) A 15-minute review call is scheduled for the day the completed template returns, focused on testing the weakest answers — typically success metrics and out-of-scope items.
