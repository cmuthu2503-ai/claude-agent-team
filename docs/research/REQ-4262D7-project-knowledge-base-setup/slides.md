# Slide 3: The Problem
- Most KBs decay within 12 months
- Content goes stale, search breaks, trust collapses
- Teams that mirror org charts in KB see adoption collapse (Stravito 2026)
- AI agents now read the KB — bad structure = hallucinations
Speaker notes: The diagnosis is consistent across every 2026 report we reviewed. Failure is rarely about the tool — it's about structure and ownership. And there's a new twist: AI agents are now the second-biggest consumer of your KB after humans.

---

# Slide 4: What Changed in 2026
- KBs are no longer just for humans — Copilot, Claude, Glean read them too
- "AI-ready" = atomic articles, metadata, clear chunking
- RAG quality is bottlenecked by KB structure, not model size
- Unstructured prose is the #1 cause of agent hallucinations (Rezolve.ai)
Speaker notes: This is the single biggest shift since 2024. If you're investing in an internal AI agent or Copilot rollout, the KB is now infrastructure for that agent. Structure quality propagates directly to answer quality.

---

# Slide 5: Platform Landscape
- Notion — 100M users, best editor, premium price
- Confluence — Atlassian-native, Rovo AI bundled, best price/value
- Obsidian — free, local, Markdown — solo/privacy use
- SharePoint — default if you already pay for M365 + Copilot
Speaker notes: These are the four serious contenders. Google Drive folders and GitHub Wikis exist but break down quickly past 20 docs or for non-engineers.

---

# Slide 6: Cost Reality (20-person team, annual)
- Notion Business + AI: ~$4,800
- Confluence Standard + Rovo AI: ~$1,300
- Obsidian + Sync: ~$1,000
- SharePoint: bundled in M365 (effectively $0 incremental)
Speaker notes: Confluence is dramatically cheaper because Rovo AI is bundled, not an add-on. This is the biggest pricing shift of 2026 and the main reason Confluence wins our weighted score.

---

# Slide 7: Weighted Comparison
- Confluence: 8.3/10 — best for Atlassian shops
- Notion: 7.8/10 — best for cross-functional teams
- SharePoint: 7.0/10 — best if already M365
- Obsidian: 6.3/10 — best for solo/privacy
Speaker notes: Scoring weights ease-of-use, cost, AI readiness, collaboration, dev integration, search, and lock-in risk. Confluence and Notion are close — pick based on ecosystem, not score.

---

# Slide 8: Recommendation
- Atlassian/Jira shop → Confluence
- Cross-functional or non-technical team → Notion
- Solo/privacy → Obsidian
- M365 + Copilot shop → SharePoint
Speaker notes: The decision tree is genuinely this simple. Don't overthink the platform — what matters is what you do *after* you pick it.

---

# Slide 9: Information Architecture
- 5–8 top-level categories — organized by TASK, not team
- Max 3 levels deep — enforce the 3-click rule
- Article template: title-as-question, prereqs, steps, owner, last-reviewed
- One r/msp team cut duplicate docs by 40% by switching to task-based IA
Speaker notes: This is the single highest-leverage decision you'll make. Categories like "Onboarding," "Deploying," "Debugging" — not "Engineering," "Marketing," "Ops." The latter feels logical but kills adoption.

---

# Slide 10: Governance That Survives
- ONE named curator per category — not a committee
- Quarterly review on the calendar — not "as needed"
- Quarterly KPIs: % articles reviewed, stale-article count, Slack-question deflection
- KBs without a named owner decay within 6 months (Bloomfire)
Speaker notes: Shared ownership = no ownership. If you remember one thing from this deck: name a curator before writing the first article. Put their review on the calendar before launch.

---

# Slide 11: Three-Week Rollout
- Week 1: Define scope, audit existing scattered docs
- Week 2: Pick platform, set up IA, start seed articles
- Week 3: Finish 10–20 seed articles, launch with internal "marketing"
- Ongoing: Quarterly curator review, gap-fill from Slack questions
Speaker notes: Resist the urge to launch with 80 thin articles. 10–20 high-quality articles answering your top questions builds trust. Trust drives adoption. Adoption drives contribution. Then it grows organically.
